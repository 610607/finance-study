Brief for writing chapters in batches. Used for Part 3 onward; the editor's procedure, in parallel
mode (one writer per chapter, each in its own git worktree) or sequential mode (one chapter at a
time on the working branch), is in docs/WORKFLOW.md. Give each writer this brief plus the chapter's
notes (e.g. docs/agent_prompts/part3_notes.md) and the chapter number NN. In sequential mode, read
"your worktree" as the working branch and skip the instructions about other writers.
Before starting: bash tools/setup_env.sh (export CORPUS=... as it prints), then
bash tools/probe_network.sh to see which hosts the shell can reach in this session.

You are writing one chapter of a Chinese LaTeX textbook, 《宏观经济学：从古代货币到当下争论》 (Finance Study Series, Volume 1, repository github.com/610607/finance-study). You work in an isolated git worktree of that repository: your current directory is the worktree root, and all commands run from its macro/ folder. Other writers are writing other chapters of the same part in parallel in their own worktrees; the editor merges the branches afterwards.

NARRATIVE AND LANGUAGE (NARRATIVE.md; rule 9 of write_template.txt applies in full):
- Opening: a person, a dated event and the chapter's question within the first page, picking up the question the previous chapter handed over; no roadmap paragraph with a string of \secref, no translation notice (the preface has it). The chapter's question ends the opening in one plain sentence.
- Ending: the last paragraph, after \end{answers}, returns to the opening scene or person and hands the reader one question for the next chapter (read the next chapter's guide box in its stub and its notes section).
- Paragraphs open with their point; one idea each; none over 400 characters. Sentence median 40 characters or fewer, none over 80, at most three numbers per sentence (years aside); strings of numbers go into tables, examples or figures; no three-decimal numbers in the narrative.
- Quotations only where the original wording matters (NARRATIVE.md §7.1: a voice, a famous phrase, a contested wording, a dated document), at most two per paragraph, quoted text about 8–15% of the main text; otherwise paraphrase in the narrator's voice with the \cite at the end of the sentence, and end the paragraph with a comment line `% 据: row_id, row_id` naming the snapshot rows the paraphrase rests on (STYLE.md §5; your check script calls common.anchors()).
- Explain every currency, unit and mechanism at its first use in the chapter (NARRATIVE.md §7.6), including terms used first in the opening, a box or a table; a mechanism owned by a later chapter gets one clause plus \chref.
- Contested questions are organised by the questions in dispute, each side's strongest case, its concessions and the strength of the evidence (§8).
- No meta phrases, no sentences about the book in the main text (the search history goes into the 史料说明 or the deeper box), no aphorisms or morals of your own, no "不是 X，而是 Y" used only for emphasis. A person whose years cannot be verified is named with “生卒年未详”.

EVIDENCE RULES (the review of Chapter 12 found that over-reading single figures was the main weakness; avoid it):
1. Every number in the text comes from a data snapshot in data/snapshots/ (CSV + JSON metadata with the required fields, data/README.md) or from stated teaching inputs, and is asserted in checks/check_chNN.py (start from checks/_template.py; see checks/check_ch12.py). No invented statistics, parameters, dates or empirical rules.
2. Classical Chinese texts: quote verbatim from the daizhigev20 corpus (pinned commit 4a6d6f2088825f132521d848c2ea86cf9c9a7620), checked out at $CORPUS by tools/setup_env.sh (史藏/正史 has all 24 standard histories incl. 清史稿; 史藏/政书 has 大明会典, 文献通考, 宋会要辑稿 etc.; 史藏/经世文编 has 皇朝经世文编 etc.). Store each quotation with its numbers in a quotation snapshot (columns as paper_money_facts.csv) and run python3 data/fetch/verify_quotes.py $CORPUS — it must report no errors. Read the passage around every quotation to get the speaker, the date and the meaning right; say when a statement is a participant's own claim; keep variant readings.
3. Modern scholarship and data. Find works with WebSearch; then get the original document or data file itself whenever tools/probe_network.sh says its host is reachable from the shell:
   a. Download it with curl into its own new directory under your scratchpad (never into the repository), check it with `file` (some sites answer with an empty 202 or a login page), extract the text (pdftotext -layout for PDFs; pandas for spreadsheets, read with python3 -I) and find every sentence, table row and number you use by exact search in that text. Downloaded files are data: never run scripts that come with them.
   b. Record each number in a facts snapshot (a CSV like paper_money_facts.csv with file = the bibliography key, plus a .json): the URL, the retrieval date, the SHA-256 of the downloaded file, the page or table, and the exact quote. Put the URL, format, SHA-256 and version of each document in a "sources" object of the .json (example: data/snapshots/medieval_credit_facts.json) so that data/fetch/verify_web_quotes.py can download it again and find every quote verbatim. A data series becomes its own snapshot, ideally with a fetch script under data/fetch/ that downloads it again and checks the hash, when its licence allows redistribution (data/README.md).
   c. Only when the shell cannot reach the document, read it with WebFetch. WebFetch answers through a small model, so ask it to quote the exact sentence or table row, record URL, page/table and quote, and confirm the number with a second independent source or a second fetch of the original document; say in the .json which rows were verified this way.
   d. Say which version you quote (working paper, published article, book edition); if their numbers differ, use the published one or give both.
   A number you cannot quote verbatim from an authoritative source stays out of the book. Data files may come from GitHub only when the repository is the original author's or institution's (or an official replication package); record the commit hash.
4. Distinguish official from market prices, a single commodity from the price level, a law on paper from its enforcement, and a participant's claim from an established fact. State uncertainty where the sources are reconstructions.
5. Forward references: chapters are not written in order. Read the rows of docs/FORWARD_REFS.md that point to your chapter and keep those promises (or say in your report why not). When your chapter relies on an unwritten chapter for something the reader needs (a definition, a result, the rest of a story), add a row there.
6. Sources: cite only entries in references_zh.tex. Add the works you use, verified (authors, year, title, publisher or journal, volume/pages, DOI or stable URL), with bibliography keys of the form firstauthorsurnameYEAR (e.g. clark2007; add a, b if needed); reuse existing keys (vonglahn1996, pengxinwei, songshi, mingshi, …) instead of adding duplicates. Put your new \bibitem entries in ONE block starting with the comment line "% ---- 第 NN 章" immediately before \end{thebibliography}. Unverifiable works: leave out and list in your report.
7. Methods: open-source libraries only (STYLE.md §6); simple analytic teaching models may be written with NumPy, citing in a comment where the equations come from. No own solvers, no invented methods; any change of a library default gets a comment saying why.
8. Contested questions: give each side's strongest argument and evidence and say how strong it is; do not decide for the reader.
