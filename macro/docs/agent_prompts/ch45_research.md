## 2. Facts read (confirm each on the document)

- fomcropa1979: the 1979 ranges set in February: M-1 1½–4½%, M-2 5–8%, M-3 6–9%, bank credit 7½–10½%; the M-1
  range adjusted for ATS and NOW accounts was equivalent to 3–6%; M-1 grew at annual rates of about 7½% and 9½% in
  the second and third quarters; “most members strongly supported a shift in the conduct of open market
  operations to an approach placing emphasis on supplying the volume of bank reserves estimated to be consistent
  with the desired rates of growth in monetary aggregates, while permitting much greater fluctuations in the
  federal funds rate than heretofore”; a few members preferred to keep targeting the funds rate; the principal
  reason: “greater assurance that the Committee's objectives for monetary growth could be achieved”, because
  “in the present environment of rapid inflation, estimates of the relationship among interest rates, monetary
  growth, and economic activity had become less reliable than before”; members recognized the link between
  reserves and the aggregates “was not precise”. The instruction: restrain reserves to a pace consistent with
  September–December growth of about 4½% in M-1 and 7½% in M-2 and M-3, provided the funds rate stayed within
  11½–15½%. Vote: Volcker, Balles, Black, Coldwell, Kimbrel, Mayo, Partee, Rice, Schultz, Mrs. Teeters, Wallich and
  Timlen (alternate) for, none against. After the meeting the Board raised the discount rate from 11% to 12% and
  set an 8% marginal reserve requirement on increases in managed liabilities (large CDs under a year, Eurodollar
  borrowings, repurchase agreements, federal funds from non-members).

  the shorter-period ones can move output too. Any rival explanation must meet two tests (why major movements in

## Friedman's rule in his own words: friedman1968 (already in docs/agent_prompts/ch44_research.md; same file, SHA-256 f3f15eb64eed66d7f0f5d209687d0d8d82dc97bb1ef9344b478e03712d891190)

- p. 15: of the guides the authority can control (exchange rates, a price index, “the quantity of a monetary

  slower and varies, so aiming at it directly would make policy “a source of economic disturbance because of false
  stops and starts”; “I believe that a monetary total is the best currently available immediate guide or criterion
  for monetary policy—and I believe that it matters much less which particular total is chosen than that one be
  chosen.” Floating exchange rates for the US (“the 5 per cent or so of our resources” in foreign trade).
- p. 16: “My own prescription is still that the monetary authority go all the way in avoiding such swings by
  adopting publicly the policy of achieving a steady rate of growth in a specified monetary total.” The rate
  matters less than “the adoption of some stated and known rate”; his own estimate for rough price stability:
  “something like a 3 to 5 per cent per year rate of growth in currency plus all commercial bank deposits or a
  slightly lower rate of growth in currency plus demand deposits only”; footnote 6: perhaps 2 per cent for the
  broader total (“The Optimum Quantity of Money”, then unpublished). A fixed rate giving moderate inflation or
  deflation would be better than “wide and erratic perturbations”.
- p. 12: the Mill quotation on money as a machine that “only exerts a distinct and independent influence of its
  own when it gets out of order” (Mill, public domain; Friedman's framing short phrases only).
